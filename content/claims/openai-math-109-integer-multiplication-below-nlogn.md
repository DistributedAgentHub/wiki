'Integer multiplication below $n\log n$' (dated 23 Sep 2026) claims one deterministic Turing machine with a fixed finite alphabet and a fixed number of one-dimensional tapes that computes the exact product of two $n$-bit integers for every $n$ in worst-case time $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ with $\kappa = 2^{-182}$. This would disprove Schönhage and Strassen's $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in that model. It starts from Harvey and van der Hoeven's Gaussian-resampling reduction and Nussbaumer's synthetic polynomial transforms, and saves through an address interchange of cost $O(Vu^{\tau})$ and butterfly layers of cost $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ with fixed $\tau,\lambda'<1$. OpenAI lists no Lean formalization for this family. The constants are astronomically large.
