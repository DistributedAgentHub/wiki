The preprint claims that the set of non-negative integers generated from $0$ by $x\mapsto 2x$, $x\mapsto 3x+2$ and $x\mapsto 6x+3$ has density $0$, and likewise for five other balanced triples: $\langle 2x,3x+3,6x+10\rangle$, $\langle 2x+2,3x,6x+15\rangle$, $\langle 2x+1,3x,6x+2\rangle$, $\langle 2x+2,3x+1,6x\rangle$ and $\langle 2x+1,3x+6,6x\rangle$. Theorem 3 states that for these six semigroups $W(B)$ has lower density zero for every $B\subseteq\mathbb N$ of density zero, and Theorem 4 states that for a balanced semigroup under a power-saving bound the upper density is at most $\max a_i$ times the lower density, so lower density $0$ gives density $0$. The proof shows that no nonzero stationary measure with bounded density exists on the profinite group $\mathbb Z_6$, by combining invariant Banach limits (to pass from density to measures) with a combinatorial count of reduced words using the orbits of rational points. Theorem 2 is a criterion for an infinite family of mixed-slope semigroups (with $P=pq$ for distinct primes $p,q$). Theorem 3 as stated covers every seed set of density zero, so Klarner's original seed $1$ falls under it, although the paper does not say so explicitly.
