Let λ be the Liouville function. The logarithmically averaged Chowla conjecture of order k asserts that for all distinct shifts $h_1,\dots,h_k$, $\sum_{n\le x}\frac{\lambda(n+h_1)\cdots\lambda(n+h_k)}{n}=o(\log x)$. It is a theorem for k = 2 (Tao) and for every odd k (Tao–Teräväinen); the problem is the first even case k = 4, and then all even k. By Tao's equivalence theorem, the conjecture for all k is equivalent to the logarithmically averaged Sarnak conjecture and to local higher-order Fourier (Gowers) uniformity of λ: for each fixed s, $\int_X^{2X}\|\lambda\|_{U^{s}[x,x+H]}\,dx=o(X)$ in logarithmic average, for H tending to infinity arbitrarily slowly. A thesis-scale target is k = 4, or local U² (Fourier) uniformity for H = H(X) → ∞ arbitrarily slowly.

# State of the art

**Open for even k ≥ 4.** Tao (arXiv:1509.05422, 2016) proved the logarithmically averaged two-point case with the entropy decrement argument; Helfgott–Radziwiłł (2021) and Pilatte (arXiv:2310.19357) made it quantitative, Pilatte with a saving (log x)^(−c). Tao and Teräväinen (arXiv:1708.02610, Duke 2019; shorter proof arXiv:1710.02112) proved every odd order, where a parity symmetry kills the structured part, and deduced the predicted logarithmic density of all length-3 sign patterns of λ and length-4 patterns of μ. Matomäki–Radziwiłł–Tao (arXiv:1509.01545) showed earlier that all eight length-3 sign patterns of λ occur with positive lower natural density. For even k the obstruction is a possible correlation of λ with nilsequences on short intervals. Matomäki–Radziwiłł–Tao (arXiv:1812.01224) proved local Fourier uniformity on average for H ≥ X^θ, and Matomäki–Radziwiłł–Tao–Teräväinen–Ziegler (arXiv:2007.15644, Annals 2023) higher-order uniformity in the same range; the conjecture needs H growing arbitrarily slowly. Tao (arXiv:1605.04628) proved the equivalence of the logarithmic Chowla and Sarnak conjectures. OpenAI's 6 October 2026 release (github.com/openai/math, family 007; unrefereed, with a Lean formalization listed) claims the unweighted two-point case with a (log x)^(−c) saving; it does not address k ≥ 3 without weights or even k ≥ 4 with weights.

# Applications

None practical. The k-point Chowla conjecture underlies sign-pattern and prime-tuple heuristics and, through Sarnak’s conjecture, Möbius disjointness for zero-entropy dynamical systems. Methods (entropy decrement, short-interval uniformity of multiplicative functions) are reused in ergodic theory and additive combinatorics.

# Plain explanation

For each whole number, count its prime factors, repeats included. Give the number a plus sign if the count is even and a minus sign if odd. So 6 is two primes, plus; 8 is three, minus; 7 is one, minus. The resulting string of plus and minus signs looks like random coin flips.

The Chowla conjecture makes this precise. It says that if you multiply the signs of several numbers at fixed distances apart, say n, n plus 1, n plus 2, n plus 3, then over many starting points n the products average out to zero, just as multiplying independent coin flips would.

This is a deep statement: it says that factoring a number tells you essentially nothing about the factoring of its neighbours. It is connected to deep questions about primes, because the signs carry information about prime factorizations.

Progress: Terence Tao proved a form of the two-number case in 2016 (with a special weighted average), and with Joni Teräväinen the case of any odd number of positions. The first unsolved case is four numbers in a row. For odd counts there is a built-in symmetry that kills the hard part. For even counts, that symmetry is missing, and you can't tell if the pattern could hide a hidden structure.

The coin-flip analogy has limits: the signs are fixed numbers, not truly random, so any proof has to show they behave randomly in a precise sense.

# Student explanation

**The Liouville function** $\lambda(n)=(-1)^{\Omega(n)}$, where $\Omega(n)$ is the number of prime factors of $n$ counted with multiplicity, takes values $\pm1$. It is closely tied to the Möbius function and to primes: $\sum_{n\le x}\lambda(n)=o(x)$ is equivalent to the prime number theorem. The **Chowla conjecture** says that for distinct shifts $h_1,\dots,h_k$, the correlation $\sum_{n\le x}\lambda(n+h_1)\cdots\lambda(n+h_k)=o(x)$. The **logarithmically averaged** version replaces the plain average with weights $1/n$:
$\sum_{n\le x}\frac{\lambda(n+h_1)\cdots\lambda(n+h_k)}{n}=o(\log x).$

**What is proved.**

- $k=2$: Tao (arXiv 1509.05422, 2016), using the **entropy decrement argument**. Helfgott–Radziwiłł (2021) and Pilatte (arXiv 2310.19357) made it quantitative; Pilatte gave a saving $(\log x)^{-c}$.
- Every odd $k$: Tao and Teräväinen (arXiv 1708.02610, Duke 2019; shorter proof arXiv 1710.02112). For odd $k$, there is a parity symmetry that kills the 'structured' part of the correlation, so only the random-like part remains. They deduced the predicted logarithmic density of all length-3 sign patterns of $\lambda$ and length-4 patterns of $\mu$.
- Matomäki–Radziwiłł–Tao (arXiv 1509.01545) had shown earlier that all eight length-3 sign patterns of $\lambda$ occur with positive lower natural density.

**The open case.** The problem is the first even case $k=4$, then all even $k$.

**Equivalences.** By Tao's equivalence theorem, the logarithmic Chowla conjecture for all $k$ is equivalent to the logarithmic **Sarnak conjecture** (that $\lambda$ is uncorrelated with every deterministic sequence, meaning one of zero entropy) and to **local higher-order Fourier (Gowers) uniformity** of $\lambda$: for each fixed $s$, an average of $\|\lambda\|_{U^s[x,x+H]}$ over $x$ is small in logarithmic density. So proving Chowla for even orders is the same as showing that $\lambda$ has no hidden polynomial-phase structure at all orders.

**Why even order is the obstacle.** For even $k$, the sign symmetry that helps in the odd case disappears; the correlation can be dominated by a 'structured' component, which is a correlation of $\lambda$ with a nilsequence (a sequence generated by a unipotent flow on a nilmanifold). Excluding that requires input beyond what entropy decrement and the Matomäki–Radziwiłł method provide, such as strong forms of Type II sum estimates.

**Unrefereed claim.** An October 2026 OpenAI release claims the _unweighted_ two-point case with a power-of-log saving; it does not address even $k\ge4$.

**Connection to the parity barrier.** The sieve parity problem is essentially a statement that $\lambda$ can't be detected by sieves; Chowla-type results are one of the few routes to circumvent that.
