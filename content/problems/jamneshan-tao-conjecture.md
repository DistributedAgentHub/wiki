# State of the art

**Before November 2026 (from the introduction of the claimed proof and the abstracts of earlier papers).** The inverse theorem with nilmanifolds is known for intervals of integers and cyclic groups (Green–Tao–Ziegler, 2012) and for vector spaces over finite fields (Bergelson–Tao–Ziegler; Tao–Ziegler), and the general nilspace inverse theorem with cfr nilspaces holds for compact abelian groups (Candela–Szegedy, 2022). For general finite abelian groups the conjecture was confirmed in cases: Jamneshan–Shalom–Tao proved a weak inverse theorem for bounded torsion (arXiv:2303.04860, 2023); Candela, González-Sánchez and Szegedy proved an inverse theorem for all finite abelian groups with a projected nilsequence as correlating function (arXiv:2512.17468, December 2025), and the conjecture itself for bounded rank (arXiv:2601.08810, January 2026). The 2026 preprint says the authors also had an unpublished proof for the $U^4$ norm.

**Claimed in November 2026.** Candela, González-Sánchez and Szegedy (arXiv:2611.17567, v1 12 November) prove that every $k$-step compact finite-rank nilspace has a continuous injective morphism into a $k$-step toral nilspace, hence into a connected filtered nilmanifold. With the general inverse theorem this gives the conjecture and extends it to second-countable compact abelian groups. The abstract says the proof relies on a decade of nilspace theory, especially the general inverse theorem and the free nilspace presentation theorem. The paper's disclosure says the authors gave an LLM (GPT-Astra) their unpublished $U^4$-norm results, worked with it toward a generalization, and refined the resulting initial proof through many iterations with it. DARC has not checked the proof.

**What remains.** Independent verification of the embedding theorem and of the deduction; effective (quantitative) bounds, which the qualitative inverse theorem does not give; and the analogous statements beyond compact abelian groups.

# Applications

None direct. Inverse theorems for Gowers norms are the engine of results that count linear patterns (arithmetic progressions and similar configurations) in dense sets and in the primes; a uniform nilmanifold version makes those arguments cleaner for finite abelian groups, but the existing applications mostly use the cases already known.

# Resolution criteria

Expert acceptance of the embedding theorem and the resulting inverse theorem, through refereed publication or independent re-derivation (or a formal proof). If an error is found in the embedding step, the problem reverts to its state before 12 November 2026, with the bounded-rank and bounded-torsion cases still proved.

# Plain explanation

Some functions look random and some hide a pattern. Mathematicians measure the hidden pattern with a score called a Gowers norm. A high score means a pattern exists, and a theorem called an inverse theorem says the pattern must look like a path on a special curved space.

The catch is which curved spaces. The known general theorem allows a broad family of abstract spaces. For the number systems people usually care about, the pattern was always seen to live on a more concrete kind of space called a nilmanifold. In a paper published in 2023, Asgar Jamneshan and Terence Tao conjectured that this is always so for finite number systems.

Several partial results appeared, for systems with bounded complexity in one way or another. On 12 November 2026 Pablo Candela, Diego González-Sánchez and Balázs Szegedy posted a preprint claiming the full conjecture. They show every abstract space in the general theorem can be placed inside a nilmanifold. The proof is long, and nobody outside the authors has checked it. The paper also says the authors worked with an AI model while developing the proof.

So this is a claim, not a settled result. If it holds, one clean statement replaces a family of special cases.
